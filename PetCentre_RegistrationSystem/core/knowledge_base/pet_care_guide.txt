Vaccination Schedule

Vaccinations help protect pets from dangerous diseases.

Dogs vaccination schedule: At 6-8 weeks, dogs need the DHPP vaccine and deworming. At 10-12 weeks, dogs need a second DHPP vaccine and the Leptospirosis vaccine. At 14-16 weeks, dogs need the final DHPP booster and the Rabies vaccine. Every year after that, dogs need booster vaccinations and a regular health checkup.

Cats vaccination schedule: At 6-8 weeks, cats need the FVRCP vaccine. At 10-12 weeks, cats need a second FVRCP vaccine. At 14-16 weeks, cats need the Rabies vaccine. Every year after that, cats need booster vaccinations.

Nutrition Guidelines

Pets require a balanced diet containing proteins, healthy fats, vitamins, minerals, and clean drinking water.

Dogs should generally eat two meals per day after six months of age.

Cats require diets rich in animal protein because they are obligate carnivores.

Avoid overfeeding because obesity can lead to diabetes, arthritis, and heart disease.

Fresh water should always be available.

Foods That Are Dangerous

Never feed pets: chocolate, grapes, raisins, onions, garlic, alcohol, coffee, tea, xylitol, avocado, macadamia nuts, or raw bread dough. These foods may cause poisoning and require immediate veterinary attention.

Common Symptoms: Vomiting

Possible causes of vomiting in pets include eating spoiled food, infection, motion sickness, and food allergies. Seek veterinary care if vomiting continues for more than 24 hours or contains blood.

Common Symptoms: Diarrhea

Possible causes of diarrhea in pets include sudden diet change, parasites, infection, and stress. Ensure hydration and visit a veterinarian if symptoms continue.

Common Symptoms: Fever

Signs of fever in pets include warm ears, loss of appetite, low activity, and shivering. A normal dog temperature ranges from 38°C to 39.2°C.

Common Symptoms: Coughing

Possible causes of coughing in pets include kennel cough, allergies, heart disease, and respiratory infection. Persistent coughing requires veterinary examination.

Skin Problems

Common skin conditions in pets include fleas, tick infestation, allergic dermatitis, ringworm, and mange. Signs include itching, redness, hair loss, and scratching.

Parasite Prevention

Internal parasites include roundworms, tapeworms, and hookworms. External parasites include fleas, ticks, and mites. Pets should receive regular deworming and parasite prevention according to veterinary recommendations.

Grooming

Dogs should be brushed regularly to remove loose hair. Cats should also be brushed, especially long-haired breeds. Trim nails every 3-4 weeks. Clean ears regularly. Brush teeth several times each week. Bathing frequency depends on breed and coat type.

Exercise

Dogs require daily exercise based on breed and age. Walking helps maintain healthy weight and improves mental stimulation. Cats benefit from interactive toys and climbing structures.

Emergency First Aid

Seek immediate veterinary care if a pet has: difficulty breathing, heavy bleeding, broken bones, continuous seizures, collapse, heat stroke, or poison ingestion.

While transporting the pet: keep them calm, avoid unnecessary movement, apply gentle pressure to bleeding wounds, and do not give human medicine unless instructed by a veterinarian.

Common Diseases: Canine Parvovirus

Symptoms of Canine Parvovirus include bloody diarrhea, vomiting, fever, and weakness. Requires immediate veterinary treatment.

Common Diseases: Kennel Cough

Symptoms of Kennel Cough include dry cough, sneezing, and mild fever. Usually spreads between dogs in close contact.

Common Diseases: Ear Infection

Symptoms of an ear infection include head shaking, ear odor, redness, and scratching ears. Requires veterinary examination.

Common Diseases: Urinary Tract Infection

Symptoms of a urinary tract infection include frequent urination, blood in urine, and pain while urinating. Needs veterinary diagnosis and antibiotics if bacterial.

Medicine Safety

Never give pets human medications without veterinary approval. Common human medicines such as ibuprofen, paracetamol, and aspirin can be toxic to pets. Always follow dosage instructions provided by a veterinarian.

Pet Health Tips

Provide fresh water daily. Feed high-quality food. Maintain regular exercise. Keep vaccinations updated. Schedule annual health checkups. Brush teeth regularly. Groom the coat. Prevent fleas and ticks. Deworm regularly. Observe changes in appetite or behavior.

Frequently Asked Question: How often should I feed my dog?

Adult dogs usually eat twice daily.

Frequently Asked Question: How much water should my pet drink?

Pets should always have unlimited access to clean drinking water.

Frequently Asked Question: Can cats drink milk?

Most adult cats are lactose intolerant. Water is the best choice.

Frequently Asked Question: How often should vaccinations be updated?

Most booster vaccinations are administered annually, depending on veterinary recommendations.

Frequently Asked Question: When should I visit a veterinarian immediately?

Visit immediately if your pet has: difficulty breathing, continuous vomiting, heavy bleeding, seizures, collapse, poison ingestion, or loss of consciousness.
